2. CURRENT APPLICATION MODEL

The application has three ordered workspaces:

THREADS -> SOUND DESK -> VISUALISER

Threads owns channel creation, sine-source programming, construction nodes, Channel Out wiring, and the contextual Monitor.

Sound Desk owns routability display, canonical X/Y placement, X-axis stereo panning, and per-channel relative Live Trim.

Visualiser retains routing inspection. Threads, Sound Desk, and Visualiser now share the same Play/Pause/Stop session transport and elapsed timer.

3. SOURCE PLACEMENT AND ROUTING

- Each unplaced sine player exposes PLACE SOURCE.
- Placement creates exactly one stable channel-bound, output-only source node in Threads.
- A placed source can connect only to its matching Channel Out.
- Placing transfers the full player out of Inputs & Channels, keeping the sidebar uncluttered.
- Selecting the placed node exposes frequency, level, and Start/Stop in Inspector.
- Removing the node returns the player to Inputs & Channels.
- Removing a channel cleans only that channel's source, wire, route, and audio branch.
- Duplicate channel-bound source placement is rejected.

4. AUDIO OWNERSHIP AND ISOLATION

One application AudioContext owns one shared session gate and one shared master.

Every stable channel owns an independent:

- sine oscillator and source envelope;
- programmed gain;
- relative Live Trim gain;
- analyser;
- stereo panner;
- frequency, level, trim, pan, routability, and active state;
- scoped disposal lifecycle.

Graph:

SOURCE -> ENVELOPE -> PROGRAMMED GAIN -> LIVE TRIM -> ANALYSER -> STEREO PANNER -> SESSION GATE -> MASTER -> DEVICE

Channel edits, starts, stops, disconnections, deletion, and recreation do not rebuild or disturb peer channels.

5. CHANNEL OUT DISCONNECTION

Disconnecting a sounding source from Channel Out now smoothly silences only that channel through its existing Live Trim stage.

The oscillator is preserved, the programmed level and trim are unchanged, peer channels are unaffected, and reconnecting restores the previous relative trim without source reconstruction.

6. SOUND DESK SPATIAL AND LIVE CONTROL

- Canonical X positions -2, -1, 0, 1, 2 map linearly to stereo pan -1, -0.5, 0, 0.5, 1.
- Y remains intentionally acoustically neutral.
- Live Trim is relative to the programmed Threads level and is bounded from -100% to +16%.
- -100% is mute; resetting to 0 restores the exact programmed level.
- Parameter changes use short smoothing ramps.

7. MONITOR AND COMPOSITE OSCILLOSCOPE

Monitor text remains contextual to the focused channel while the oscilloscope renders one combined trace of every currently sounding, routed channel.

The composite makes phase interaction, reinforcement, cancellation, and beating visible in one view. Unrouted, muted, paused, and stopped channels are excluded because they are not audible.

The waveform loop reuses fixed buffers and does not recreate audio nodes or allocate new analyser buffers each frame.

Sound Desk mute status is now explicit. When the focused active, wired channel has Live Trim at -100%, Threads Monitor reports:

- SD MUTED;
- Sound Desk Live Trim -100%;
- the unchanged programmed Threads level.

This replaces the misleading NO SIGNAL / ACTIVE combination shown in the 18:22 session screenshot. If peer channels remain audible, their composite waveform continues while the focused channel is identified as SD muted.

8. SESSION TRANSPORT AND TIMER

The same Play/Pause/Stop transport is available in Threads, Sound Desk, and Visualiser.

- PLAY starts or resumes the shared session gate and elapsed timer.
- PAUSE smoothly gates playback to silence and freezes elapsed time.
- STOP smoothly gates playback to silence and resets elapsed time to 00:00:00.

Pause and Stop preserve active source eligibility, programming, routes, trim, pan, and constructed audio branches. A subsequent Play resumes without oscillator reconstruction. The former separate timer controls have been removed, eliminating manual one-second trigger skew.
